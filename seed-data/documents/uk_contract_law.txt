PRINCIPLES OF UK CONTRACT LAW

1. FORMATION OF CONTRACT

1.1. Essential Elements

For a contract to be valid under English law, the following elements must be present:
- Offer: A clear and definite proposal made by one party
- Acceptance: Unqualified agreement to all terms of the offer
- Consideration: Something of value exchanged between parties
- Intention to create legal relations: Presumed in commercial agreements
- Certainty of terms: Key terms must be sufficiently certain

1.2. Offer and Acceptance

The "mirror image rule" requires that acceptance must be an unconditional agreement to all terms of the offer. A counter-offer constitutes a rejection of the original offer and creates a new offer.

Communication of acceptance:
- Postal rule: Acceptance effective when posted
- Instantaneous communications: Effective when received
- Electronic communications: Electronic Commerce Regulations apply

2. TERMS OF CONTRACT

2.1. Express Terms

Terms explicitly agreed by the parties, either:
- In writing (preferred for commercial contracts)
- Orally agreed
- Partly written, partly oral

2.2. Implied Terms

Terms may be implied by:
- Statute (e.g., Sale of Goods Act 1979)
- Custom or trade usage
- The courts (to give business efficacy)

2.3. Conditions, Warranties, and Innominate Terms

- Conditions: Fundamental terms; breach allows termination and damages
- Warranties: Minor terms; breach allows damages only
- Innominate terms: Consequences depend on severity of breach

3. REMEDIES FOR BREACH

3.1. Damages

The primary remedy for breach of contract:
- Compensatory damages: Put claimant in position as if contract performed
- Expectation loss: Loss of bargain
- Reliance loss: Wasted expenditure
- Consequential damages: Foreseeable losses

Remoteness test (Hadley v Baxendale):
- Naturally arising from breach, or
- Within reasonable contemplation of parties at contract formation

3.2. Specific Performance

Equitable remedy ordering party to perform contractual obligations:
- Discretionary
- Available where damages inadequate
- Not available for personal service contracts

3.3. Injunctions

Court order requiring party to do or refrain from doing something:
- Prohibitory: Restrains breach of negative covenant
- Mandatory: Requires positive action

4. LIMITATION OF LIABILITY

4.1. Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977 (UCTA)

Key provisions:
- Cannot exclude liability for death or personal injury caused by negligence
- Other negligence liability can only be excluded if "reasonable"
- In business-to-business contracts, exclusion clauses subject to reasonableness test

4.2. Consumer Rights Act 2015

For consumer contracts:
- Unfair terms not binding on consumer
- Core terms exempt if transparent and prominent
- Grey list of potentially unfair terms

5. FORCE MAJEURE AND FRUSTRATION

5.1. Force Majeure

Contractual provision excusing performance when extraordinary events occur:
- Must be expressly included in contract
- Scope depends on drafting
- Notice requirements typically apply

5.2. Frustration

Common law doctrine terminating contract when:
- Supervening event occurs
- Event is beyond parties' control
- Event makes performance impossible, illegal, or radically different

Effects:
- Contract automatically discharged
- Law Reform (Frustrated Contracts) Act 1943 governs financial consequences

6. DISPUTE RESOLUTION

6.1. Jurisdiction Clauses

Parties may agree which courts have jurisdiction:
- Exclusive: Only specified courts
- Non-exclusive: Specified courts plus others

6.2. Governing Law

Choice of law clause specifies applicable law:
- Party autonomy recognized
- Rome I Regulation (EU Regulation 593/2008) for EU contracts
- Common law rules post-Brexit

6.3. Arbitration

Alternative to court litigation:
- Arbitration Act 1996 governs
- London as major international arbitration seat
- LCIA, ICC commonly used institutions
